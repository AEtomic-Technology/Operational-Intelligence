# Operational Intelligence Glossary

This glossary contains the working definitions used throughout the Aetomic Operational Intelligence research programme.

These definitions provide consistency across research papers, applied studies and technical work. They will be refined as the body of research develops.

---

## Operational Intelligence

The organisational capability to perceive changing conditions, interpret their significance in context, and support timely, proportionate and accountable action.

Operational Intelligence extends beyond the collection of data. It connects evidence, context, operational state, authority, objectives and organisational memory so that information can be understood and acted upon appropriately.

---

## Operational State

The current condition of an organisation, operation, team, asset or environment at a particular point in time, as understood through available evidence and relevant context.

Operational state is not defined by a single data point. It emerges from the relationship between conditions, events, resources, obligations, risks and intended outcomes.

---

## Operational Stability

The condition in which an operation remains within acceptable boundaries and continues to achieve its intended purpose despite normal variation, disruption or uncertainty.

Stability does not mean that nothing changes. It means that change remains understood, manageable and within the organisation’s capacity to respond.

---

## Operational Drift

The gradual movement of an operation away from its intended state, required standard or acceptable boundary.

Operational drift often develops through small deviations, deferred actions, weakened controls, changing assumptions or normalised workarounds rather than through a single major failure.

---

## Operational Health

An assessment of an operation’s ability to sustain its intended outcomes, manage current demands and respond to emerging change.

Operational health may be influenced by stability, resource adequacy, unresolved exceptions, control effectiveness, information quality and decision readiness.

---

## Signal

An observation, event or pattern that may indicate a meaningful change in operational state.

A signal becomes operationally significant only when interpreted in context. The same observation may be routine in one environment and material in another.

---

## Material Change

A change significant enough to affect operational objectives, risk, priorities, resource requirements, decision ownership or required action.

Not every change is material. Determining materiality requires context and judgement.

---

## Evidence

Information that can support, challenge or refine an understanding of operational reality.

Evidence may include records, observations, sensor data, communications, documented actions and verified human reports. Its value depends on relevance, reliability, timeliness and context.

---

## Context

The surrounding conditions that give operational information meaning.

Context may include location, time, role, authority, history, dependencies, objectives, constraints, risk and the current operational state.

---

## Confidence

The assessed degree of certainty attached to an observation, interpretation or recommendation.

Confidence should reflect the quality and completeness of available evidence, the consistency of signals and the presence of unresolved uncertainty.

---

## Authority

The recognised right to make a decision, direct action, approve an exception or accept operational risk within a defined scope.

Authority is distinct from competence. A person may understand a situation without possessing the authority to decide, while an authorised decision-maker may require expert advice before acting.

---

## Decision Ownership

The explicit assignment of responsibility for determining what should be done when judgement, approval or acceptance of risk is required.

Clear decision ownership reduces delay, duplication and unaccountable escalation.

---

## Operational Tempo

The rate at which operational conditions change and at which the organisation must observe, decide and act.

Operational tempo may vary significantly between steady-state activity, emerging disruption and active crisis.

---

## Steady State

A condition in which an operation is functioning within expected parameters and no material intervention is presently required.

Steady state does not imply inactivity. Routine work, monitoring and control remain necessary to preserve stability.

---

## Operational Memory

The accumulated organisational record of events, decisions, actions, outcomes and learned relationships that can inform future understanding and action.

Operational memory should preserve not only what happened, but also the context in which decisions were made.

---

## Human Judgement

The ability to interpret incomplete information, weigh competing considerations and make proportionate decisions in context.

Operational Intelligence systems should strengthen human judgement by improving visibility, continuity and reasoning support rather than obscuring or displacing accountability.

---

## Status of Definitions

These are working definitions and form part of an evolving research programme. Substantive revisions will be recorded through GitHub’s version history.
